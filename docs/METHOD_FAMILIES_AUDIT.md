# Method family audit

The 57 initial labels are a discovery vocabulary, not 57 independent elegant strategies. Additional narrow entries discovered during pair review are listed below. Every definition below was assessed for its meaning and holdout suitability. Eight actual shortlisted questions were inspected to expose overlap and keyword errors. Pair correctness and weaker-model findability remain separate review tasks.

## Holdout contract

A family denotes the decisive mechanism that makes the elegant route shorter. Execution primitives such as expansion, casework and solving a quadratic can occur in both roles and should usually be shared across splits. A topic such as functional equations or graph theory does not specify a mechanism. Mixed labels must be narrowed before declaring a whole-family holdout.

For each held-out mechanism, predeclare a conservative exclusion neighborhood and inspect all retained source routes of potential training problems. Exclude questions with equivalent encodings, renamed identities, or the held-out mechanism embedded in a longer route. Closely related labels are not necessarily equivalent; the groups below identify where a narrow distinction needs justification. Do not use indiscriminate transitive graph closure: this would collapse much of mathematics. The current saved partitions are preserved and should be treated as provisional.

Exactly two reference routes is a useful export invariant. Exactly two possible mathematical solution paths is neither established by the sources nor generally provable. Select one short route and one independently understandable longer route; preserve other source routes for leakage review.

## Mechanism overlap neighborhoods

- **gap compositions:** gap_placement, stars_bars, bijection.
- **overlap counting:** complement, inclusion_exclusion.
- **state counting:** recurrence, dynamic_programming, generating_functions, matrices.
- **coefficient root algebra:** vieta, newton_sums, polynomial_remainder.
- **polynomial recovery:** interpolation, finite_differences, binomial.
- **modular power filters:** modular, crt, fermat_euler, roots_unity.
- **prime exponents:** prime_factors, valuations.
- **analytic geometry:** coordinates, vectors, complex_geometry.
- **triangle incidence ratios:** similarity, area, projective.
- **circle transforms:** power_point, cyclic, inversion.
- **trigonometric representations:** trigonometry, trig_substitution.
- **inequality certificates:** am_gm, cauchy, rearrangement, bounds, calculus.
- **sum cancellation:** telescoping, geometric_series.
- **structural counting:** symmetry, double_counting, linearity, bijection.

## Examples inspected

- **1984 AIME 11** (`885c64d34027fe012e30`): Gap choice versus residual-gap casework share the same placement encoding. Useful execution contrast, weak whole-method novelty. Ordinary numeric substitution is falsely labeled as change of variables in an alternate route.
- **2018 AMC 12B 8** (`039296d5b546007acb04`): Bounding regex matches region bounded; actual short route is centroid homothety, with coordinates as alternate. Add homothety as strategy label.
- **2011 AIME II 4** (`2623a47b9529cd912dd0`): Menelaus, mass points and similar-triangle construction share triangle incidence ratios. Projective umbrella is too broad; missing mass-point label prevents semantic leakage checks.
- **2020 AMC 10A 14** (`5fcc292c15b8372003d8`): Low-degree symmetric polynomial identities and Newton recurrence overlap closely; labeling Newton sums held out while Vieta/coefficient identities train requires an explicit narrow holdout claim.
- **1986 AIME 11** (`0700faf295976c90f6c3`): Geometric-series reduction, binomial coefficient summation and hockey-stick are different derivations but closely linked identities. Matrix lexical label needs inspection; no matrix strategy in first three human routes.
- **2001 AIME I 15** (`2c430d178ddfdb683aff`): Cube dual and coloring produce graph representation not necessarily a graph-specific theorem. One tempting short source route claims unique extension and its own comment supplies counterexamples; brevity alone does not establish validity.
- **2001 AIME I 14** (`9256d9a8aafffc273311`): Three-state recurrence evaluated by table is dynamic programming despite only recurrence keyword. A scalar recurrence can be shorter, but deriving state compression is the relevant insight.
- **2022 AIME I 15** (`747e091346eb1453017d`): Algebraic squaring is fairly short and systematic; trigonometric routes involve representation and branch reasoning. Do not assume a named advanced method is always the elegant role.

## Pair-level requirements

- State the shortcut insight in a sentence that does not merely name a theorem. Identify the trigger in the question that makes the insight discoverable.
- State why the elementary route is easier to initiate and execute: a bounded search, a visible case partition, a standard equation procedure, or explicit state transitions. More words alone do not establish this.
- Record actual symbolic/arithmetic steps and branching burden, not just token length. Count independently invented constructions and prerequisites. A short but obscure unexplained step fails the naturalness criterion.
- Require a verifier that checks the original requested answer and relevant domain/branches. An answer-only verifier certifies the output, not the two derivations.
- Empirically test stronger and weaker models before claiming a capability gap; expert judgments of discoverability are hypotheses.

## Refinements discovered during pair review

Additional manually applied entries extend the initial vocabulary; original
regex labels and saved assignments remain unchanged. Adding a precise name does
not establish independence from neighboring mechanisms.

| Entry | Mechanism and boundary |
| --- | --- |
| Simson line and converse | Use the equivalence between a point lying on a triangle’s circumcircle and collinearity of its three perpendicular feet onto the side lines. Establish all three feet and nondegenerate triangle conditions. |
| Heron’s area formula | Use A squared = s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c), with s the semiperimeter and a,b,c satisfying the strict triangle inequalities. This is an area-computation tool. |
| Shoelace area formula | Compute polygon area from the signed cross-product sum over boundary-ordered coordinates, checking simple polygon and vertex order. |
| Pythagorean theorem | For a stated right triangle, use the sum-of-squares relation between its legs and hypotenuse, with correct length and sign choices. |
| Quadratic discriminant constraints | Impose nonnegative, zero, or square discriminant as appropriate to constrain real, repeated, or rational roots of a quadratic. Check leading-coefficient and domain conditions. |
| Angle-bisector ratio theorem | Use the ratio in which an internal angle bisector divides the opposite side, equal to the adjacent-side ratio; distinguish external signed ratios. |
| Stewart’s cevian theorem | Relate a cevian length and its two base segments to adjacent side lengths through Stewart’s theorem, verifying segment assignments. |
| Lifting the exponent (LTE) | Compute a prime valuation of a difference or sum of powers through LTE after checking the prime-divisibility, coprimality, exponent-parity and p=2 conditions for the particular formula. Preliminary multiplicative-order reduction may be required. |
| Ptolemy’s theorem | In a cyclic quadrilateral, diagonal product equals the sum of opposite-side products; verify cyclicity and vertex order. |
| Pick’s theorem | Relate area to boundary/interior points of a simple lattice polygon; verify the lattice and boundary counts. |
| Isogonal-cevian ratio product | Relate two angle-bisector-reflected cevians through the adjacent-side squared ratio; verify the isogonality conditions. |
| Farey neighbors / mediant bound | Use determinant-one reduced neighbors to bound denominators of intermediate fractions. Related to Bézout, not prime valuations. |
| Burnside fixed-point averaging | Count finite group-action orbits through mean fixed-point counts; specify the action and every group element type. |
| Reinforced-urn exchangeability | Show sequence probabilities depend on color counts rather than draw order under a specified reinforcement rule. |
| Möbius inversion / divisor convolution | Reverse a divisor sum with the arithmetic Möbius function; related to inclusion–exclusion but not geometric inversion. |
| Mass points / barycentric balance | Assign balancing vertex masses and use the lever rule at cevians. Related to triangle ratios, not a synonym for projective transformations. |
| Hook-length formula | Bijection to standard Young tableaux of a specified shape, then factorial divided by hook lengths. Establish shape and ordering conditions. |
| Homothety / dilation | Identify one center and scale ratio for a common dilation; transfer lengths and areas. Narrower than generic similarity. |
| Apollonius distance-ratio circle | Use the circle locus for a fixed non-unit ratio of distances to two points; derive center/radius and exploit it. |
| Symmetric reciprocal-power identities | Evaluate powers of x and 1/x through symmetric sums/recurrences, avoiding explicit roots. Overlaps Newton sums and symmetric polynomial identities. |
